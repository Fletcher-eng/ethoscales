What is Hobbesian Absolutism?

Hobbesian Absolutism is a political ideology in the Classical family. Hobbes argued that people are roughly equal in strength and wants, so without a power to overawe them they live in constant fear and conflict. Reason tells them to give up their natural liberty by covenant and authorise a sovereign to act for all. That sovereign, whether one man or an assembly, must be absolute and undivided: it makes the law, judges disputes, and decides which doctrines and what worship are allowed. Subjects may not resist it, except to save their own lives. Authority rests on consent and fear, not on divine appointment.

Without a common power, a war of all against all.

Defined from: Thomas Hobbes, Leviathan (1651)
