What is Catholic Integralism?

Catholic Integralism is a political ideology in the Reactionary family. The encyclical and its attached list of eighty condemned propositions reject the leading ideas of nineteenth century liberalism. Among the errors named are that Church and state should be separated, that Catholicism need no longer be the religion of the state, that every person may choose a religion by the light of reason alone, and that the Pope should reconcile himself with progress, liberalism and modern civilisation. Integralists take from it that civil power must serve the spiritual end of human life.

The state owes public recognition to the Catholic faith.

Defined from: Pope Pius IX, Quanta Cura and the Syllabus of Errors (1864)
