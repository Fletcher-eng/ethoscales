What is Public Choice Constitutionalism?

Public Choice Constitutionalism is a political ideology in the Liberal family. Buchanan and Tullock analyse politics with the tools of economics, treating voters, legislators and officials as individuals who pursue their own ends as they do in markets. Simple majority rule has no special standing: it lets a majority pass costs on to a minority and invites vote trading. Only unanimous agreement fully protects everyone, so the constitution, the stage at which people choose rules without knowing how they will fare under them, should set how large a majority each kind of collective decision needs.

Voters and officials pursue their own interests, so the rules matter more than the rulers.

Defined from: James M. Buchanan and Gordon Tullock, The Calculus of Consent (1962)
