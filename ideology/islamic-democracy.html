What is Islamic Democracy?

Islamic Democracy is a political ideology in the Religious family. Ghannouchi argued that human beings are God's trustees on earth, so authority belongs to the community and rulers hold office only by its consent. Consultation, shura, is binding and takes the modern form of elections, parties, a free press and the peaceful handover of power. He grounded civil liberties and the rights of non Muslim citizens in the Quran and the purposes of Islamic law, which remains the frame of reference for legislation. Against Qutb, he denied that democracy is alien to Islam.

Elections, pluralism and rights grounded in Islamic sources.

Defined from: Rached Ghannouchi, Public Freedoms in the Islamic State (1993)
