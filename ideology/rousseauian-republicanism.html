What is Rousseauian Republicanism?

Rousseauian Republicanism is a political ideology in the Classical family. Rousseau held that legitimate authority comes only from a pact in which each person gives himself wholly to the community and obeys laws he has helped to make. Sovereignty belongs to the people as a body, expresses the general will, and can be neither divided nor handed to representatives; citizens must vote the laws in person, which suits small states. Whoever refuses the general will may be compelled to obey it. He wanted no citizen rich enough to buy another, and a simple civil religion to bind citizens to their duties.

Man is born free, and everywhere he is in chains.

Defined from: Jean-Jacques Rousseau, The Social Contract (1762)
