What is Gaullism?

Gaullism is a political ideology in the Conservative family. At Bayeux de Gaulle argued that France's repeated collapses came from party rivalry and a weak executive. Executive power should not proceed from parliament but from a head of state who stands above the parties, is chosen by a body wider than parliament, appoints the government, and guarantees national independence and the continuity of the state. Parliament keeps two chambers and makes the law. These ideas shaped the constitution of 1958.

A strong head of state above the parties, and an independent France.

Defined from: Charles de Gaulle, The Bayeux Speech (1946)
