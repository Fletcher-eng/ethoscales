What is Steady State Economics?

Steady State Economics is a political ideology in the Green family. Daly treated the economy as a subsystem of a finite biosphere and argued that it cannot grow for ever. The goal should be a constant population and a constant stock of physical goods, maintained with the lowest possible flow of energy and materials. He proposed three institutions: government auctions of quotas that cap resource extraction, transferable licences that stabilise births, and minimum and maximum limits on income and wealth. Within those limits, markets and private property still allocate resources. Development then means better, not more.

Defined from: Herman Daly, Steady-State Economics (1977)
