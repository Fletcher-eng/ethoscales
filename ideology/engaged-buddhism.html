What is Engaged Buddhism?

Engaged Buddhism is a political ideology in the Religious family. Written in the middle of the Vietnam War, the book argues that Buddhism cannot stay in the monastery while people are bombed and starved: compassion has to act in the world. Nhất Hạnh held that for most Vietnamese the war was about independence, not ideology, and that foreign firepower only strengthened the insurgency. He presented the Buddhists as a peaceful majority refusing both sides, and called for a ceasefire, an end to the bombing and the withdrawal of foreign troops.

Practice that goes out to meet suffering in society.

Defined from: Thích Nhất Hạnh, Vietnam: Lotus in a Sea of Fire (1967)
