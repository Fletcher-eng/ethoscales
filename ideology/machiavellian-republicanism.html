What is Machiavellian Republicanism?

Machiavellian Republicanism is a political ideology in the Classical family. Reading the history of Rome, Machiavelli concluded that republics are stronger and more lasting than principalities, and that the people are safer guardians of liberty than the nobles. The quarrels between plebeians and senate, far from weakening Rome, produced the laws that kept it free. A republic needs a mixed constitution, citizens who fight in their own armies instead of mercenaries, and a civic religion that binds them to the common good. It must expand or decay, and be pulled back to its founding principles from time to time.

A free republic kept alive by conflict, law and its own arms.

Defined from: Niccolò Machiavelli, Discourses on Livy (1531)
